# EAGLE 3.1 draft head for Apertus-v1.5-8B (thinking off, 10 epochs)

This is a speculative-decoding draft head (EAGLE-3 architecture, "EAGLE 3.1"
training recipe) for
[swiss-ai/Apertus-v1.5-8B](https://huggingface.co/swiss-ai/Apertus-v1.5-8B) at
revision `a411d838600baf0e3635a3daf66fb7c55fc97bb6`. It is trained for
**thinking-off** requests.

It was trained on exactly the data and split of the DSpark e10 drafter (TODO:
link the released DSpark repo), for a like-for-like comparison. This is the best
checkpoint of the completed 10-epoch run (step 251,955 of 254,480). Internal
names: run `e31-opb-thinkoff-stage1`, head `e31-opb-thinkoff-stage1-se`,
"EAGLE e10".

Speculative decoding does not change what the model outputs. The target model
verifies every drafted token, so only the speed changes.

## Intended use

- Use it to speed up serving of Apertus-v1.5-8B in vLLM when thinking is off.
- In vLLM it works with the standard `eagle3` method: no new algorithm, only the
  Apertus loader patches below. That makes it the easier head to adopt where
  DSpark is not available.
- At k=3 and under load it is as fast as DSpark. At long drafts and low load,
  DSpark is faster.
- Do not use it with other targets or revisions, or with thinking-on requests.
  It is not a standalone language model.

## Architecture

- `LlamaForCausalLMEagle3`: 1 decoder layer (hidden size 4,096, MLP 14,336,
  32 heads, 8 KV heads).
- It reads 3 auxiliary target hidden states. In vLLM's indexing these are
  layers 2, 16 and 29, which are the outputs of Hugging Face decoder layers 1,
  15 and 28.
- It uses the full 131,072-token vocabulary, with no draft-vocabulary mapping.
- The input embedding is **not included**. vLLM shares it from the target.
- `lm_head.weight` **is included**. It is the target's LM head, frozen during
  training.
- Weights are bf16, in `model.safetensors` (1.66 GB). It has 1.37 B parameters
  stored, of which 294 M were trained.

## Training procedure

- Trainer: TorchSpec `6c042a87140a84d13839e341ece2c5c3ada918bc`, with the
  Apertus patches, run through the `apertus_eagle` rollout pipeline of the
  project repository (harness `4c322349be6a74553659c086a997e930bf536d2e`).
  Target features were computed online from the frozen bf16 target.
- Loss: forward KL against the target distribution, test-time-training length 7,
  position weights 0.8^k.
- Batch: 64 sequences per optimizer step (8 ranks × 8 accumulation steps,
  micro-batch 1).
- Optimizer: AdamW (fp32 master weights), learning rate 1e-4, 1.5% warmup,
  cosine decay to 0 over 254,480 steps (10 epochs), max grad norm 0.5, no
  weight decay. Embedding and LM head are frozen.
- Hardware: GH200 nodes, 1 node for epochs 1 to 4 and 2 nodes after that.
  Training saw 16.06 B tokens over 26 Slurm jobs (3552036 to 3696348).
- Config: `configs/train-e31-opb-thinkoff.yaml` (sha256 `79d87053…`, as recorded
  in `training/provenance.json`), `configs/draft-e31-config.json` and the
  target contract `configs/contract.json`.

Held-out simulated acceptance length at 7 draft tokens: **3.988** at the best
step. Per-position accuracy at that step:

| Position | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | 0.877 | 0.861 | 0.853 | 0.847 | 0.842 | 0.838 | 0.834 |

By epoch: 3.448 (1), 3.651 (2), 3.751 (3), 3.878 (5), 3.955 (7), 3.985 (9),
3.987 (final).

The export was validated by reloading the exported tensors. They were
bit-identical, with identical rollout accuracy (`export-report.json`).

## How to serve with vLLM

The measurements used the Swiss AI vLLM build for Apertus 1.5, at vLLM commit
`a601a9d998ddeb488f0c17e8512874b116aa7658` (Clariden SquashFS
`vllm_apertus_1.5_release-arm64.sqsh`, sha256
`f5fc017ff794fa8d80b73dd9a788028a1872dbfc2e21c0de043fb7738c11a0b5`). That build
needs three source patches, all included in `serving-patches/`:

| Patch | Why |
|---|---|
| `vllm-apertus-eagle3-aux-layers.patch` | `SupportsEagle3` helpers fail on Apertus 1.5's inner language-model wrapper before the auxiliary layers can be set |
| `vllm-apertus-eagle-mrv2-inner.patch` | Model runner v2's EAGLE loader dereferences `.model` on the same wrapper |
| `vllm-apertus-image-token.patch` | The draft proposer reads `image_token_index`, which Apertus 1.5 names `image_token_id` |

The measured engine arguments:

```bash
vllm serve swiss-ai/Apertus-v1.5-8B \
  --dtype bfloat16 --tensor-parallel-size 1 --max-model-len 32768 \
  --gpu-memory-utilization 0.8 --max-num-batched-tokens 16384 --max-num-seqs 256 \
  --no-enable-prefix-caching \
  --speculative-config '{"method": "eagle3", "model": "<this repo>", "num_speculative_tokens": 3}'
```

- **Thinking must be off.** Pass `chat_template_kwargs={"enable_thinking": false}`.
- Choosing the draft length:
  - Use **k=3** as the general setting. It is never below plain serving on
    summarization or Arena-Hard.
  - Use k=5 for in-distribution traffic at low to medium load.
  - Use k=1 for chat-heavy traffic above C≈128.

## Results

All measurements are on one GH200 with bf16 and TP=1, greedy, thinking off.
Each speedup is output tokens/s divided by a plain-serving arm of the same
target in the same job.

jobs 3709466 and 3709795, 2026-10-20):

| 1 | 1.66x | 2.13x | 2.42x | 2.59x | 2.70x | 2.74x |
| 8 | 1.64x | 1.96x | 2.29x | 2.40x | 2.45x | 2.50x |
| 32 | 1.48x | 1.62x | 1.88x | 1.94x | 1.83x | 1.93x |
| 64 | 1.41x | 1.51x | 1.70x | 1.58x | 1.58x | 1.56x |
| 128 | 1.28x | 1.34x | 1.43x | 1.32x | 1.33x | 1.29x |
| 256 | 1.19x | 1.24x | 1.28x | 1.22x | 1.21x | 1.15x |

## Limitations

- **Non-English text.** The training prompts are 99.55% English. Expect low
  acceptance and little or no speedup on other languages.
  - The deployed DSpark head, trained on the same data, gains only 1.1 to 1.6x
    at C=1 on 12 non-English languages and is 8 to 33% slower than plain at
    C=128.
  - EAGLE e10 was not measured per language. TODO: add it if the headline run
    includes it.
- **High concurrency.** When the GPU is compute-bound, rejected drafts cost
  throughput.
  - On chat, k=3 is 4 to 6% slower than plain from C=128.
  - At k=7, chat and summarization are below plain from C=64.
  - Use k≤3 under load.
- **Long drafts.** EAGLE runs one drafter pass per draft token. At k=7 and low
  load, DSpark is therefore 25 to 27% faster on equal acceptance.
- **Thinking on** is not covered. A separate thinking-on run exists but is not
  part of this release.
- **Hardware and software.** Results are for GH200 with the vLLM build and
  patches above.
