# Licence audit: training data of the Apertus 1.5 8B drafters

Status 2026-11-22. This audit is a release prerequisite for the two thinking-off
drafters of Apertus-v1.5-8B (DSpark e10 and EAGLE 3.1 e10). It was raised in the
2026-11-20 huddle: every subpart of the training data must be as permissive as
Apache-2.0 or MIT. Not allowed: CC-NC, AGPL, unlicensed data, custom licences.

The Swiss AI handbook pages could not be read from here (they redirect to a
login):
- `.../engineering/data/data_guidelines/#license-checking-in-short`
- `.../engineering/data/external-data-collaboration/`

So this audit applies the rule as Rob stated it in the huddle. The leads should
check it against the handbook.

## Verdict

**Not clean. The drafters must not be published as-is under the "every subpart
permissive" rule.**

The Open-PerfectBlend card says "All of these datasets use either an Apache 2.0
or MIT license". That is true only one level down. One of its eight sources,
`openbmb/UltraInteract_sft` (MIT), is itself an aggregate. It contains two
non-commercial datasets:

| Subpart | Licence | Rows in the OPB revision we trained on |
|---|---|---|
| ReClor (LSAT/GMAT logical reasoning) | Non-commercial research only (the official download's password is `for_non-commercial_research_purpose_only`) | **7,159** |
| TabMWP (table math word problems) | **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** | **at least 4,568** |

These counts are exact matches of each dataset's prompt template in the OPB
parquet files at the pinned revision (script and query below). Together that
is 11,727 rows, 0.83% of OPB.

Further problems, which the leads must rule on:
- **Share-alike** prompts (CC BY-SA 4.0): HotpotQA (2,586 rows) and
  WikiTableQuestions (1,738).
- **Prompts without a licence:** ShareGPT user prompts reached the corpus
  through UltraFeedback (about 20k of its 64k prompts come from ShareGPT) and
  through AutoIF's query augmentation.
- **Problem statements owned by third parties,** relicensed by intermediate
  datasets:
  - Codeforces and AtCoder problems in CodeContests and TACO.
  - MAA competition problems in MATH.
  - An Alpaca (CC BY-NC 4.0) lineage behind CodeAlpaca-20k: its card says
    CC BY 4.0, but it was made with the Alpaca pipeline.

This is the "licence washing" pattern Rob warned about. The top-level labels
(Apache-2.0 and MIT) hide NC and unlicensed subparts.

What reduces the exposure (for the leads, not a clearance):
- **Only the prompts survive.** Every assistant turn was regenerated by
  Apertus-v1.5-8B (Apache-2.0). None of the original answers was trained on,
  including the GPT-3.5/4 answers that most of these datasets consist of.
- **The drafters do not contain the text.** They are 0.3 to 1.4 B-parameter
  heads that predict Apertus' next tokens from Apertus' hidden states.
- The affected prompts are under 1% of rows (about 1.1% counting share-alike).
  About 90% of them fell into the training split.

The rule as stated ("every subpart") does not have a de-minimis exception. So
the options are:

1. **Retrain without the bad subparts (recommended if the rule is strict).**
   - Drop UltraInteract rows of ReClor and TabMWP. Optionally also drop
     HotpotQA, WikiTableQuestions, and the UltraFeedback and AutoIF rows whose
     prompts come from ShareGPT.
   - The regenerated answers already exist, so filtering the corpus needs no
     new generation.
   - Cost is the training runs: DSpark about 800 GPU-h for 10 epochs, EAGLE
     about 16 B tokens on 1 to 2 nodes. Fine-tuning the e10 heads on the
     filtered corpus would not remove the earlier exposure.
2. **The leads or legal accept the residual risk.** They would rely on the
   prompt-only use, the share below 1%, and the drafter not reproducing text.
   The model card then names the subparts and their licences.
3. **Publish under a non-commercial or research-only licence.** This would be
   inconsistent with the Apache-2.0 target, and the CSCS inference platform may
   not accept it.

## Source table

How to read it:
- Licences come from each Hugging Face card's `license` field. Card revisions
  were fetched 2026-11-22 through the Hub API; the sha is listed where relevant.
  Where no card exists, the licence comes from the GitHub repository's licence
  file or README.
- Verdicts: **OK** means permissive (MIT, Apache-2.0, CC BY, ODC-By). **FLAG**
  means a decision is needed (share-alike, unlicensed or third-party-owned
  text). **BLOCK** means not permissive (NC or custom research-only).
- "Used" means what reached the drafters. In every case only the user prompts
  were used, because every answer was regenerated by Apertus.

## How the counts were made

- Source counts: `results/8b/eagle/data-perfectblend/check.json` (job of
  `apertus_eagle.perfectblend_check`, revision `af60f3c`).
- UltraInteract subpart counts: Hugging Face datasets-server statistics of the
  `dataset` column of `openbmb/UltraInteract_sft` (2026-11-22).
- OPB template counts: `tools/release/opb_subsource_counts.py` (output
  `docs/release/opb-subsource-counts.jsonl`), a DuckDB query over the six parquet files of
  `mlabonne/open-perfectblend` at `af60f3c`, read over HTTPS (nothing stored).
  It ran 2026-11-22 on the first message of each row, with these patterns:
  - ReClor: `Given the context and corresponding question, choose the correct answer from the options`
  - TabMWP: `Given the following table:` + `Table title:`
  - HotpotQA/StrategyQA: `wikipedia_search(query: str)`
  - WikiTableQuestions: `Given the following table (only the first 5 rows are shown)`

  All matches are in the UltraInteract slice; every other source has zero. The
  TabMWP pattern misses UltraInteract's TabMWP rows that lack a table title, so
  its count is a lower bound.
- Licences: Hugging Face Hub API `cardData.license` and the cards' text; GitHub
  `license` API and READMEs (2026-11-22).

## Open questions for the leads

1. **Strictness.** Does "every subpart must be permissive" apply to prompts only,
   when every answer was regenerated by our own Apache-2.0 model? If yes, the
   ReClor and TabMWP rows (0.83%) block release, and we retrain on a filtered
   corpus (option 1).
2. **Share-alike and unlicensed prompts.** Are CC BY-SA (HotpotQA,
   WikiTableQuestions) and ShareGPT-derived prompts (UltraFeedback, AutoIF,
   Evol-Instruct V2) acceptable? If not, the filter for option 1 grows. ShareGPT
   rows are hard to identify inside OPB, because OPB does not keep
   UltraFeedback's per-prompt source; it needs a join with
   `openbmb/UltraFeedback` on the prompt text.
3. **Third-party problem text.** Are competition problems (MAA, Codeforces,
   AtCoder, LSAT/GMAT) relicensed by intermediate datasets acceptable?
4. **Gating.** Should both repos be gated with the Apertus v1.5 AUP, like the
   target? This is required for EAGLE, because it carries a copy of the Apertus
   LM head.
5. **Kaggle origin.** The LMSYS 55k data was first released for a Kaggle
   competition. Is the Apache-2.0 relicensing by its owner (LMSYS/lmarena-ai) on
   Hugging Face sufficient?
6. **Is a retrain worth it?** Option 1 retrains both heads, or only the deployed
   DSpark head. Who signs off on the filtered corpus definition?
