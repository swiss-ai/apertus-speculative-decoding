# Apertus 1.5 speculative decoding: leads review (draft)

Slide content for the review with the leads that Rob proposed on 2026-11-20. Its purpose is to decide whether to release the
8B drafters on Hugging Face. All numbers come from pushed, pinned reports (see Sources).

- Approve the public release of the Apertus 1.5 8B DSpark drafter (and possibly EAGLE): HF hub, then the CSCS inference platform.
- Decide the licence issue in slide 6, which blocks release as things stand.
- Choose the HF org/repo names (proposal: `swiss-ai/Apertus-v1.5-8B-DSpark`).

## 2. What it is

- A small draft model proposes k tokens and Apertus verifies them in one forward pass. **The output is identical to plain
  decoding; only the speed changes.**
- DSpark e10: a 5-layer drafter trained for 10 epochs on Apertus-regenerated Open-PerfectBlend. It has been in production on the
  serving platform since 2026-10-31 (`Apertus-v1.5-8B-dspark-e10-k3`).
- A 70B drafter is in training (16 nodes on Beverin, 10 epochs, past epoch 4 of 10 as of 2026-11-20).

## 3. Headline: speedup on the production engine

Production flags, plain vs DSpark on the same GPU, 28 task slices × 32 prompts, greedy decoding. Each cell is the best draft length k.

| Traffic | C=1 | C=8 | C=32 | C=128 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All slices | **2.0x** | **1.8x** | **1.5x** | 1.0x |
| English math / code / chat-SFT (ID) | **4.0x** | **3.6x** | **2.5x** | 1.4x |
| Near-ID (JSON, tools, Arena-Hard) | 3.1x | 2.6x | 1.9x | 1.2x |
| OOD tasks (summarisation, long context, …) | 1.7x | 1.5x | 1.3x | 1.0x |
| Non-English (12 languages) | 1.3x | 1.3x | 1.2x | 0.84x |

- Time per output token at C=1 goes from 5.8 ms to 2.7 ms (k=7); time to first token rises by 4–6 ms.
- The deployed k=3 setting gives 1.73 / 1.69 / 1.47 / 1.03x (all slices). The live pod matches controlled runs within ±7%.

## 4. Where it does not help

- Under heavy load (C=128), non-English traffic is 16% slower and OOD tasks break even.
- Cause (measured, 2026-11-22): the drafter disagrees with Apertus on non-English and chat text, even on tokens where Apertus
  is confident. Its top-1 matches on 99.9% of math tokens, 77% of chat tokens and 51–75% of non-English tokens. Target entropy
  does not explain the gap; draft/target alignment explains R²=0.97 across slices.
- Mitigations: choose k by load (k=7 at low load, k=3 at high load), turn drafting off at saturation, and add more multilingual
  training data.

## 5. Things we tried that did not pay off

These were bounded experiments with pre-set decision rules. All are written up and posted in #swiss-ai-spec-decode.

- Multilingual domain adaptation of DSpark: +3–9%, below the bar. For EAGLE it gave +17%.
- Narrowing top-k sampling, multi-drafter trees, a shallow 1-layer drafter, the paper's scheduler, and P/D disaggregation as a
  default.
- P/D layouts: speculation on a disaggregated decode GPU pays off up to C≈256. The limit above that is KV capacity, not compute.

## 6. Release blocker: training-data licence

- Open-PerfectBlend is labelled Apache/MIT, but its `UltraInteract_sft` part contains **ReClor (non-commercial)** and
  **TabMWP (CC BY-NC-SA)** prompts: 0.83% of rows.
- Items for the leads to rule on: share-alike prompts (HotpotQA, WikiTableQuestions), unlicensed ShareGPT-derived prompts, and
  third-party problem text (MATH, Codeforces).
- All answers were regenerated by Apertus, so only the prompts carry over, which lowers the risk.
- Options:
  - (a) retrain on a filtered corpus;
  - (b) accept the risk;
  - (c) release under a restrictive licence.
- The EAGLE head embeds the Apertus LM head, so it is gated under the Apertus AUP. DSpark should be gated too.
